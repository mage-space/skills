# Marketplace rules

Rules change; when the user sells on a marketplace, their seller central help is the authority. These are the common requirements to design for.

## Amazon

**Main image**
- Pure white background (RGB 255, 255, 255).
- The product fills about 85% of the frame, whole and in focus.
- Only what the buyer receives: no props, no extra items, no accessories that aren't included.
- No text, logos, badges, borders, watermarks, or color blocks added to the image.
- A photograph or photoreal render, not a drawing.
- Square, at least 1000 px on the long side so zoom works (Mage's 2K output qualifies).

**Secondary images** may show the product in use, with props, infographics, and text, as long as they are accurate.

**A+ content** modules are wide banners and image-with-text blocks. No pricing, shipping, time-sensitive offers ("sale", "now"), guarantees or warranties, customer reviews presented as ratings, or comparisons with competitors by name. Claims must be true and supportable.

## Walmart

- Main image: white background, product only, square, the product fills most of the frame.
- No watermarks, no promotional text on the main image.
- Additional images may show lifestyle, scale, and details.

## Etsy

- No white-background requirement; the first photo is the thumbnail, cropped to about 4:3 in search. Keep the product centered with margin.
- Natural light, real context, and scale shots perform well.
- Use `4:5` or `1:1` and keep key detail in the center.

## eBay

- White or plain background preferred for the gallery image; no borders, text, or artwork.
- At least 500 px on the long side; larger is better for zoom.

## Shopify and direct-to-consumer stores

- The store sets the rules. Consistent backgrounds, aspect ratio, and product scale across the catalog matter most.
- Ask which aspect ratio the theme uses (often `1:1` or `4:5`) and keep it for the whole set.

## Truthfulness

Everything on a card must be true of the product the buyer receives. Don't invent certifications ("dermatologist tested", "organic"), ratings, awards, results, or quotes. When the user asks for claims they can't support, keep the card image-only.
